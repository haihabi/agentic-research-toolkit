# Fallback form — IEEE / society conference (SPS / ICASSP family)

> **Fallback in use.** The real review form for this venue+year could not be
> found. This form reproduces the structure common to IEEE Signal Processing
> Society conferences (ICASSP, ICIP, ICME, …) from their published editorial
> procedures. Deliverable A must state this in bold.

`last_reviewed: 2026-09-06`

`process_model: editor-mediated-referees` · `score_style: ordinal-categories` ·
`response_routing: chair-or-editor-only` · `review_model: single-anonymous`

## Process (for the run README, not the review)

Track-chair initial check (desk reject possible) → **at least three** reviewers →
reviews returned to authors → authors get **≥ 1 week for an optional rebuttal
that goes to the TC / Area Chairs and is *not* shared with the reviewers** → TC
chairs decide. One round; outcome accept / reject.

## Fields (in order)

| Field | Instructions |
|---|---|
| Summary of the paper | What is proposed and what is claimed, in your own words. |
| Strengths | Specific and detailed. |
| Weaknesses | Discrete, taxonomy-tagged items, ordered by severity. |
| Detailed comments to the authors | Section-by-section technical comments; specific and actionable. |
| Relation to prior work | Is the most relevant prior work (including classical and prior IEEE work) cited and compared? |

## Criteria — ordinal categories (pick one label per criterion; do NOT score 1–N)

| Criterion | Labels (verbatim-style) |
|---|---|
| Importance / Relevance | `Of broad interest` · `Of sufficient interest` · `Of limited interest` · `Irrelevant` |
| Paper type | `Theoretical` · `Algorithmic / methodological` · `Application / system` · `Performance evaluation` (choose what fits; it scales the experimental-validation expectation) |
| Originality / Novelty | `Very original` · `Somewhat original` · `Marginally original` · `Has been done before` (this last requires a citation and implies rejection) |
| Theoretical development | `Rigorous` · `Adequate` · `Superficial` · `Flawed` |
| Experimental validation | `Thorough` · `Adequate` · `Insufficient` · `Not applicable (theoretical paper)` — **scaled by paper type; theoretical papers may need none** |
| Clarity of presentation | `Clear` · `Mostly clear` · `Hard to follow` · `Unacceptable` (comprehension difficulties may lead to rejection) |
| Reference to prior work | `Complete` · `References missing (listed below)` · `Does not cite relevant work (implies rejection)` |
| Overall evaluation | `Definite accept` · `Probable accept` · `Borderline` · `Probable reject` · `Definite reject` |
| Award quality | `Yes` · `No` |
| Reviewer confidence | `Expert` · `Knowledgeable` · `Some familiarity` · `Educated guess` |

## Decision set

`accept` · `reject` (one round). Note in the TC-chair summary if the work would
clearly succeed after a revision the format does not allow.

## Scoring / conduct notes

- Use the whole range; give `Definite accept` / `Definite reject` when deserved
  and defensible; reserve `Borderline` for genuinely middling papers, not for
  low confidence or low effort.
- Scores must be consistent with the written comments.
- **Disregard minor formatting and English issues** — they are `presentation`
  nits, not grounds for rejection.
- Experimental-validation expectations follow the paper type; do not demand
  experiments (or deep-learning baselines) a method/theory paper never promised.
- Single-anonymous: you may see author names; do not let identity affect the
  review.
